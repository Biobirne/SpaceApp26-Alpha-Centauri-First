# Pitch script: Moon Base Commander (team 19)

The spoken script for the live pitch, slide by slide.
- **Bold lines:** say them word for word. They are the lines the jury should remember.
- **Bullets:** cues. Say them in your own words.
- **Speakers:** Florian (slides 1–3) and Cornelius (slides 4–7).
- **Time:** 90 seconds planned, 2 minutes hard limit.
- **Deck:** `pitch/index.html` (see `PITCH.md`, "Final deck and script"). One click per slide; the demos run by themselves.

Written with the SI tool Claude Code from the team's decisions. The wording is ours to change.

## The idea in one breath
**Low floor, high ceiling, wide walls.** "A 7-year-old can't lose, and a 12-year-old can't run out of things to learn."

Everything we say points back to this. Before the pitch, practise saying the three words in the same rhythm every time: the jury hears them three times (slide 1, slides 4–6, slide 7).

## The rhetoric we use, and where
| Device | Where | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Open question | Slide 1 | Puts the jury inside the game before they know what it is |
| Pause | After the question, and after "278 times" | Silence makes a number land. Count two seconds in your head |
| Contrast | Slide 2: 7 vs. 12 | The problem in one picture |
| Rule of three | Low floor, wide walls, high ceiling | Easy to remember, and it structures slides 4–6 |
| Show, don't tell | Slides 4 and 5: the demos run while you talk | Let the screen do the explaining |
| Callback | Slide 7 repeats slide 1's question | Closes the loop, so the pitch feels finished |

## Slide by slide

### 1 · Title (Florian, ~12 s)
Open with a question, then pause.

**"Imagine you're seven. You're running a base on the Moon, and the radiation outside just jumped to 278 times normal. What do you do?"**

*(two seconds of silence)*

- We're Alpha Centauri First, team 19.
- This is Moon Base Commander, our mission trainer for kids aged 7 to 12.

Other openers, if this one doesn't feel right:
- "Who here learned something from losing a game? … Most kids learn: 'I'm bad at this.'"
- "What do a 7-year-old and a 12-year-old have in common? Not much. That was our problem."

*(click)*

### 2 · The problem: 7 vs. 12 (Florian, ~12 s)
- The challenge says kids aged 7 to 12. That's a huge range.
- Point at the 7: still learning to read.
- Point at the 12: wants the real data.

**"Most learning games pick one, and lose the other. We built one game for both."**

*(click)*

### 3 · The real storm (Florian, ~15 s)
- And it starts with something real: 14 May 2024.

**"Radiation near the Moon hit 278 times normal."** *(pause)* "Measured by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter."

- Every level is a real storm like this, replayed day by day, from NASA's RadLab and DONKI data.
- Hand over: "Cornelius, show them what a kid does with that."

*(click)*

### 4 · Low floor: Call Earth (Cornelius, ~15 s)
Start talking right away. The signal takes 2.6 s and needs no commentary while it travels.

**"Low floor: anyone can start, and nobody loses."**

- No game over. After every mistake, Sam explains what happened and what to try next.
- Sam reads everything aloud, for kids who can't read yet.
- When the answer pops up: "Stuck? Call Earth. And you wait 2.6 seconds, because radio only travels at the speed of light. Just like real astronauts."

*(click)*

### 5 · Wide walls: Apollo 13 (Cornelius, ~15 s)
The mini-game plays by itself. Talk over it, and don't explain the taps.

**"Wide walls: there's so much to build, fix and explore."**

- Kids build their own base and repair it with their hands.
- When the story card appears: "Apollo 13: the square filter that didn't fit the round hole. Kids build the same fix, from bags, cardboard and tape."
- Every repair ends with a true story like this.

If time is short, cut this slide's talk to the bold line and the Apollo 13 sentence.

*(click)*

### 6 · High ceiling: one tap to the real data (Cornelius, ~12 s)
**"High ceiling: every number is a door."**

- One tap, and the simple bar becomes the real NASA measurement, with the unit and the instrument.
- And three stars are hard. Just following Mission Control isn't enough on the big storms; you need your own plan.

*(click)*

### 7 · One game for every kid (Cornelius, ~10 s)
Callback to the opening question, then the line, then stop.

- "So, what do you do when the radiation hits 278 times normal? Our players know."
- **"Low floor, high ceiling, wide walls."**
- **"A 7-year-old can't lose, and a 12-year-old can't run out of things to learn."**
- Free, on any phone, no login.
- **"Thank you."** Then stop talking. Don't add "So, yeah…".

## Delivery
- **Before the first click:** stand still, look at the jury, then ask the question. Don't start while walking up.
- **Numbers:** say "278 times" slowly. It is the only number the jury needs to remember.
- **Hand-over:** Florian ends slide 3 by looking at Cornelius. No "and now my colleague will…".
- **The demos run alone:** don't wait for them and don't narrate the clicks. If a demo is slow, keep talking and click on.
- **The mouse:** left click = next, right click = back. If something goes wrong: press R on the laptop to replay a slide, F for full screen.
- **Rehearse with a timer** at least twice. Over 90 seconds: cut slide 5 first, never speed up.

## If something breaks
- **Deck doesn't load:** check that `python3 tools/serve.py 8000` is running in the repo folder, then reload.
- **A demo freezes:** click on. Every slide also makes sense as a still picture.
- **No laptop at all:** tell the story from this script and point to the game URL: biobirne.github.io/SpaceApp26-Alpha-Centauri-First
